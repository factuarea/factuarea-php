# TransitionFulfilmentStatusRequest

Moving a delivery note along its physical axis.

The body carries the DESTINATION state, `target_status`, and — only when the
destination is a failed delivery — the reason, `failure_reason`. There is ONE
operation for the whole axis instead of one per step: the warehouse handles
it as a single control, and publishing an operation per step would give ten
endpoints for one decision.

`target_status` is one of the seven states of the physical axis: `pending`,
`picking`, `prepared`, `handed_over`, `in_transit`, `delivered` and `failed`.
Anything outside that catalog is rejected naming the field.

Belonging to the catalog is NOT the same as being reachable. Which
destinations are admitted depends on where the delivery note is right now,
and a destination the state machine does not admit from the current state is
rejected as a business-rule violation naming the current state and the
admitted ones — never as a permission problem: the caller has permission, it
is the document that does not admit the step. Delivery to the recipient is
TERMINAL and nothing moves after it; a failed delivery admits a second
attempt back into transit; and a delivery note cancelled on its commercial
side does not advance at all, which is the only rule that crosses the two
axes.

`failure_reason` is required when the destination is a failed delivery and
rejected with any other destination: with a destination that is not a failure
it would be a note nobody ever reads back, and a failed delivery with no
reason is a dead end for whoever has to decide whether to try again. It is
free text of up to 500 characters — the length of an operational note, not of
a column — and it travels with the event the transition emits.

Moving along this axis NEVER changes the commercial status of the delivery
note and never emits an event of the commercial axis: whoever subscribes to
the status of the document receives exactly what it received before this axis
existed.

The operation is declared IRREVERSIBLE as a whole, and the flag is per
OPERATION and not per destination value: two of its destinations are
irreversible — handing the goods to the carrier has an effect on a third
party, and delivery to the recipient is terminal — so the reversible steps
ask for an idempotency key as well when the policy of the credential requires
one. Over-marking costs one header; under-marking loses the guarantee on the
two steps that need it.
