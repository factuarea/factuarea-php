# AddPurchaseOrderLineRequest

Add ONE line to a purchase order that still accepts changes. The line is a COPY of the supplier offer as it stands when you add it: the supplier reference, the purchase unit, the factor that converts that unit to the base unit, the agreed cost, the minimum quantity and the lead time all come from the offer and are frozen on the line, so a later catalog change does not rewrite an order already placed and a later goods receipt converts with the agreed factor. Required: `product_id` and `quantity`. Optional: `product_variant_id`, `offer_id` (an ACTIVE offer; omitting it takes the preferred active offer of the product) and `warehouse_id` (an ACTIVE warehouse of your company; omitting it inherits the one on the order). `quantity` is in PURCHASE UNIT — the one the offer publishes, which need not be the base unit of the product — greater than zero, with at most four decimals and up to 9999999999.9999. What this body REJECTS naming the field instead of ignoring it: the unit cost (both spellings, `unit_cost` and `unit_price`) and the `tax_rate`, `retention_rate` and `surcharge_rate` percentages, which come from the chosen offer — to buy on other terms pick another offer, or send the lines with their amounts when you create the order, which is the only operation that accepts them; the `description`, which is composed from the product; the `received_quantity`, which is never writable on any route and moves on its own when a goods receipt is posted or cancelled; and the line `id`, which the order assigns and returns in the response. The line amount and the taxes derived from it have their own, narrower limit, checked against the agreed cost of the offer before the line is created. Every reference — product, variant, offer and warehouse — must belong to the company in the path: one from another company is rejected exactly like a non-existent one. An order that no longer accepts changes by its status rejects the creation of lines as a business-rule violation, not as a validation error.
